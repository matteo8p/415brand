1Key findings

  • Viktor gets eight times Adapt's website traffic, and most of it is either people typing its name or people clicking its ads. 

    viktor.com had 646K visits in July; adapt.com had 82K. But look at where Viktor's visitors come from. 48% type the address in directly. 30% click an ad (banner ads 12.5%, Google ads 9.3%, paid posts on social media 8.4%). Only 9% arrive from free Google results, meaning they searched for something and clicked a normal, unpaid link. And of that 9%, nearly nine in ten in the US had simply typed "viktor" or a misspelling of it. Google searches for "viktor ai" went from 90 a month a year ago to 5,400 in July, which is what a funding round, famous investors and a paid creator program do to name recognition. Viktor's traffic has also stopped growing: 243K in May, 640K in June, 646K in July. Similarweb Semrush Google Keyword Planner

    Why it matters for Adapt: Viktor looks eight times bigger, but the gap is name recognition that was bought, not a better product. Adapt's 82K is growing 21% a month with no paid social media and a Google Ads budget under $1,000. The searches that describe what both companies actually sell, like "slack ai agent" (320 searches a month, $11 per click), belong to nobody yet. One more thing worth knowing: by Similarweb's estimate, the most valuable search term sending visitors to adapt.com is "viktor ai", ahead of "adapt company". That ranking is by dollar value, not visitor count, and it almost certainly reflects Adapt's own ads and comparison page catching people who were looking for Viktor. That is working; it is one more reason to get that page right. Similarweb Semrush

  • Viktor spends $150K to 176K a month on Google ads and points them at people searching for ChatGPT, Claude and Slack, not at people looking for an AI employee. 

    SpyFu, a tool that estimates what companies spend on Google ads, puts Viktor's US budget at $175,585 a month, up from $98,570 in May, spread across 13,810 search terms. A second tool, Semrush, estimates $82K for July; the two tools count differently, so the truth is likely between them. Viktor's biggest paid search term is "chatgpt alternative" (12% of the clicks it pays for). After that come its own name, "cowork" and "what is claude cowork", and, per SpyFu, plain words like "openai", "slack", "saas" and "marketing". The ads send people to a page comparing Viktor with Claude Tag and to a "hire an AI employee" page. Google's public ad records show 200+ ads from Viktor's company (ZETA AI, Inc.) since Mar 12, plus 18 ads from 13 companies that aren't Viktor but send people to viktor.com, which usually means people chasing Viktor's referral commission. Adapt runs 31 Google ads and spends roughly $900 to 3,000 a month on searches like "claude cost", "hermes agent vs openclaw" and "how to build an ai agent". SpyFu Semrush Google Ads Transparency

    Why it matters for Adapt: Viktor's money is making "chatgpt alternative" and "cowork" expensive, not the searches Adapt should want. "viktor alternative" only started being searched in May, had 90 searches in July, and costs $6 to 20 per click; "viktor pricing" and "viktor credits" aren't on Viktor's list at all. Adapt already has the comparison page those searchers want to read. And the budget is going up, not down: the Google Ads lead Viktor posted on Jul 21 requires experience running "$1M+/month". Google Keyword Planner Ashby

  • Viktor's social media reach is a paid machine: creators paid up to $10K a post, referral commissions, paid consultants, and a group of big accounts that cheer each other's posts on launch day. 

    We found 84 videos about Viktor on TikTok and YouTube with 349K views between them. 29% of those views came from creators who were paid or earn a commission. Once you remove two clips from a podcast (20VC) that got 192K views on their own, 64% of what is left is paid, commission or partner content. Viktor's creator program pays $200 for a post that 1,000 to 5,000 people see, rising to $10,000 for 100,000+, or 50% more if the creator takes payment in Viktor credits instead of cash; there is no minimum follower count, and Viktor says 83% of creators take the credits. Anyone who refers a customer earns 15 to 20% of what that customer pays for a year. Consultants who set Viktor up for a client keep their own fee plus 20% of the client's spending for 12 months. On X, Viktor's new feature launch on Aug 20 produced 129 posts in two days. 31 of them came from accounts with more than 50,000 followers, and most of those were one-line replies to each other's posts within the same hours ("Excellent contribution", "noice", "The context-awareness is the killer feature"). Only 4 of 236 posts in our sample were complaints. TikTok YouTube Viktor X

    Why it matters for Adapt: Adapt has no creator, referral or commission program. Its reach comes from two paid-promotion posts on X (649K and 617K views, fewer than 100 likes each) and one paid YouTube ad. Viktor's creators are agency owners, media buyers and office managers. Nobody is being paid to talk to developers, and Viktor's ads and job listings barely mention them. The machine is worth copying, and paying in credits costs Adapt almost nothing. The audience should be the one Viktor ignores. X YouTube

  • Viktor's job ads admit most paying customers are one person using it alone, and it is hiring marketers faster than engineers to fix that. 

    Viktor has 27 open jobs: 8 in marketing and growth, 5 in product and design, 5 in operations, 4 in sales and support, and 5 in engineering. Ten of them were posted after the new sales chief arrived on Jul 29, and none of those ten is an engineering job. The job descriptions say things the marketing doesn't: "half of paying workspaces still have only one active human"; "in many paying teams today, one power user drives most of the usage"; "our biggest leak is between a user's first message to Viktor and their first scheduled task"; the automated emails that nudge new users are "run by the Head of Growth as a side quest"; and the new Head of Conversion (the person whose job is turning free users into paying ones) will oversee ad spending "scaling toward 2x volume in the next two quarters", where "every point of conversion lift is six figures of ARR". Every ad promises a "top-of-the-market salary" and none prints a number, except the New York sales job where the law requires it: $100K to 270K a year. Ashby LinkedIn

    Why it matters for Adapt: "one shared AI assistant for the whole company" is Adapt's pitch, and Viktor's own job ads say that is exactly where Viktor is weakest. The people hired to fix it (email marketing, conversion, customer success, community) are all in Warsaw and none had started when we checked. Adapt has one open job (a salesperson) and about 13 staff in San Francisco. Adapt cannot out-hire Viktor, but it can sell team-wide adoption while Viktor is still recruiting the people who would answer that objection. Ashby LinkedIn

  • Viktor doubled its cheapest paid plan, its customers can't predict their bills, and Adapt's comparison page still quotes the old price. 

    Both companies charge in "credits", pre-paid units that get used up as the AI does work. Viktor's cheapest paid plan is now $100 a month for 40,000 credits. Its own web page still says "Paid plans start at $50/month" in the hidden description Google reads, its FAQ still asks "What can I do with 20,000 credits?", and its listing in Slack's app store still says $50. The change happened between Aug 1 and 9. On G2, 7 of the 9 reviews written in August complain about credits or cost in the "dislike" box ("kind of a black box on how tokens are used", "the price tag"). Viktor has also only completed the first, lighter stage of SOC 2, the security audit that bigger companies ask vendors for; Adapt has completed the full version. Adapt's comparison page says Viktor costs "$50/mo" and works "in Slack only". Both are now wrong: Viktor added a web app on Aug 6, Discord on Aug 1, and on Aug 20 a way for other software to plug into it. Viktor Slack Marketplace G2 Adapt Viktor

    Why it matters for Adapt: Adapt's sharpest argument, a $50 starting price, a published price per credit, an itemized bill, and spending limits per person (shipped Aug 14), sits behind a page that undersells it and gets two facts wrong. Someone who Googles "viktor pricing", lands on Adapt's page and checks it against Viktor's will stop trusting it. Fix the page, then say the price out loud in ads, because none of Viktor's 238 ads mentions price, credits or security. Adapt Meta Ad Library

Prices

Viktor: Free is $0 with $100 of credits that never expire. Team is $100 a month for 40,000 credits shared by everyone in the workspace, with unused credits rolling over, top-ups when you run out, and an optional automatic top-up with a spending cap. Enterprise is priced per deal, with per-person spending limits, invoicing, a data-processing agreement and the option to keep data in Europe. Viktor says credits reflect "model work done across Anthropic, OpenAI, and others"; an outside analysis describes the pricing as the cost of the underlying AI times 1.5, aiming for a 33% margin. Adapt: Starter is $0 with $25 of credits, $100 more for connecting Slack, and up to $300 for a work email address. Pro is $50 to $5,000 a month paid up front at one cent per credit, with up to 10% bonus credits and automatic refills. Enterprise is priced per deal and comes with a dedicated Adapt engineer. Neither company charges per user. Adapt introduced the $50 starting price on May 26; Viktor doubled its starting price in early August. Viktor Adapt Latka

  • Viktor bought its way onto Google in one quarter, and the free traffic it does get is mostly people typing its name. 

    In May, viktor.com showed up in the free results for 56 different searches worldwide and spent an estimated $99K on US Google ads. The growth in free results is real but thin: in the US, 87% of Viktor's free search visitors typed "viktor", "getviktor", "getvictor", "viktor ai" or "victor ai". The homepage gets 99.9% of that traffic; the only other pages that get any are a comparison with Superhuman Go and a brand page. The 13 blog posts Viktor published Aug 12 to 23 haven't shown up in Google yet. SpyFu estimates that 88% of the clicks Viktor gets from Google are clicks on ads it paid for. Semrush SpyFu Ahrefs

    Why it matters for Adapt: the searches that describe the category are wide open. "slack ai agent" (320 a month, $11 per click), "ai employee slack", "viktor alternative" (90 a month, brand new), "viktor vs claude tag": none of these has a clear winner. The one Adapt page that brings in search visitors without being about Adapt's name is its comparison of OpenClaw and Claude Cowork, which is 17% of Adapt's free search traffic. More pages like that, aimed at Viktor, would work. Google Keyword Planner Ahrefs

Meanwhile 13 unrelated companies (shell companies, Hong Kong ad networks) run 18 Google ads that send people to viktor.com, most likely to collect Viktor's 15 to 20% referral commission.
