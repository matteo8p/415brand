# Snappy: competitor dossier

*Research date: 2026-08-30. Sources: a crawl of snappy.com (homepage, /pricing, /about-us, /press-release-archive, sitemap via Firecrawl map), TechCrunch (Jan 2025), Calcalist (Apr 2024), Fortune (May 2021), Wikipedia, Forge Global, G2, Trustpilot, RepVue, Glassdoor, and Reddit. Read `goody.md` first. Items marked **[unverified]** come from a single third-party source.*

> **The name is a trap.** "Snappy" is a common English adjective. A bare search returns Wirecutter TV reviews ("snappy performance"), Snappy Shopper (UK grocery delivery), Snappy Tots (crochet patterns), and Google's Snappy compression library. Search `snappy.com`, `"Snappy Gifts"`, `snappygifts`, `meetsnappy`, or `@snappygifts`. Never the bare word. See `../CLIENT.md`.

## The short version

Snappy is the incumbent. Ten years old, $130M raised, 9M+ gifts delivered, and **56% of the Fortune 100** as customers. On paper it is not a fair fight.

Three things complicate that picture, and all three are openings:

1. **The Series D was a down round.** $25M in April 2024 at an estimated $180–200M, roughly half the Series C valuation — after laying off close to a third of the company in January 2023.
2. **Snappy has shipped no product in 2026.** Every press release this year is workforce research or an awards win. The last product launch was the AI Gifting Assistant in October 2025.
3. **Buyers rate Snappy 4.8. Recipients rate it 1.9.** This is the single most important finding in this file.

## The numbers Snappy publishes, and where they disagree with each other

Snappy's own site carries several mutually inconsistent figures. This matters because it is the same failure mode Goody has, and neither side can throw the stone:

| Claim | Homepage | Pricing page | About page | Press release |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gifts delivered | 9M+ | — | "over 9M" | "over 7 million" (Oct 2025) |
| Countries | 150+ | 176+ | — | — |
| Gift options | 350,000+ | "more than 250,000" | — | "over 250,000" (Oct 2025) |
| Customers | "5,000+ companies" | — | "7,000+ customers served" | — |
| Fortune 100 | — | — | **56%** | **47%** (TechCrunch, Jan 2025) |

**Goody's own blog claims Snappy supports "40+ countries."** Snappy's site says 150+ or 176+. Somebody is wrong, and it is worth finding out which — a wrong claim in Goody's most-linked marketing asset is a liability if a Snappy rep finds it.

## Pricing

| Tier | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Essential** | **$0/year** | Unlimited users and gifts, 176+ countries, automated campaigns, branded messaging, Zapier/Make (8,000+ apps), on-demand swag with no inventory, fulfillment in 30+ countries, Build Your Own collections |
| **Elevated** | **$2,000/year** | Swag expert + broader on-demand catalog, **Snappy Stores**, **40+ HRIS** (ADP, Workday, BambooHR), **Slack and Teams** notifications, Chili Piper, branded unwrapping experience |
| **Enterprise** | Custom | Dedicated onboarding, custom kits and bulk with inventory storage, **Snappy API Suite**, Salesforce, SSO, custom reveal animations, **AR experiences**, in-house Creative Services |

Gift budgets from **$5 to $15,000**. Recipients can donate the gift value to charity (GetChange.io API, Make-A-Wish flagship partnership, $2M+ raised).

**Two things to read off this table:**

- **Snappy publishes a real number ($2,000/yr) and Goody does not.** Goody's Team tier says "chat with us." Snappy is more transparent than Goody at the mid tier — which is awkward given Goody's entire comparison post is an argument about pricing transparency.
- **Snappy paywalls Slack and Teams.** Gift notifications in Slack cost $2,000/year. For a distributed company, that is the single most-wanted feature in the product and it is behind a wall.

**[Goody's claim, unverified]** Goody's comparison post asserts Snappy takes a **15% processing fee on gifts**. We could not verify this on Snappy's site or pricing page. Do not put this in a report until someone confirms it — an unverified fee claim about a competitor is the kind of thing that gets a client sued, not just corrected.

## The review split — the most important thing in this file

| Surface | Rating | Volume | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| G2 (seller page) | **4.8/5** | 312 reviews | The buyer: HR, EA, ops |
| Snappy's own badge | 4.9/5 | "300+" | — |
| **[unverified]** Docket.io | 4.7/5 | "400+" | — |
| **Trustpilot (meetsnappy.com)** | **1.9/5 "Poor"** | **13 reviews** | **The recipient** |
| **[unverified]** RepVue (employees) | **2.8/5** overall | ranked #2,770 of software orgs | Sales reps who work there |

Thirteen Trustpilot reviews is a small sample and must be described that way in any report. But the *direction* is not noise, and the mechanism is obvious: **G2 is written by the person who pays, Trustpilot is written by the person who receives.** Snappy's product is judged by the buyer on ease of admin and by the recipient on whether the thing that arrived was any good.

Goody has the mirror-image exposure and should check its own recipient-side sentiment before leaning on this. But as a competitive wedge, "our recipients like us too" is a stronger argument than any feature comparison in this category, and nobody is making it.

**Corroborating recipient-side signal:** a Reddit thread in r/OPTIMUM titled *"What is a 'Snappy Gift'? Is it a scam?"* — the recipient experience is unfamiliar enough that people's first instinct is fraud. That is a brand problem no amount of Fortune 100 logos fixes.

**What G2 buyers do complain about** (via SoftwareReviews and Goody's own summary): limited employer-side customization of gift selection; per-gift costs; limited options at a given price point when you let the recipient choose; the catalog "skews corporate."

**2026 press releases, all of them:**
- Jun 3 — "Personalization is the #1 Driver of Meaningful Employee Recognition" (workforce study)
- May 26 — Two Telly Awards for "The Gifting Intervention" campaign
- May 14 — "72% of Employees Say Work Anniversary Gifts Would Make Them More Likely to Stay"

**The read:** Snappy's marketing engine is original research, and it is genuinely good at it — annual workforce studies, holiday gifting reports, birthday studies, Gen Z research with Georgia Tech and Penn State. This is category-defining content and Goody has nothing like it. But research PR is what a company runs when the product roadmap is quiet, and eight months into 2026 the roadmap has been quiet.

Meanwhile Goody shipped Company Stores, Forms, an MCP server, a Chrome extension, HubSpot, Salesforce Flow, and went from 30 to 200 HRIS integrations in the same window.

## Where Snappy beats Goody, honestly

Do not write a report that pretends otherwise:

1. **Enterprise trust.** 56% of the Fortune 100. Microsoft, HubSpot, eBay, Uber, Meta, Starbucks, T-Mobile on the homepage. Goody's logo wall is Gong, Rothy's, Intercom, SoftBank — good mid-market names, a different league.
2. **Warehousing and bulk kits.** Snappy stores inventory, does custom packaging, bulk discounts, six months free storage. Goody explicitly does not.
3. **Catalog scale.** 250,000–350,000 SKUs against Goody's 600 brands. Different unit, but the gap is real at the long tail.
4. **The unwrap experience.** Branded reveal animations, custom animations from an in-house creative studio, AR experiences at Enterprise. Goody's digital cards are good; this is theatre.
5. **Original research.** Snappy owns the "gratitude / appreciation ROI" narrative outright.
6. **Slack and Teams delivery.** Paywalled, but native. Goody routes Slack through Zapier.
7. **Charity donation as a gift option**, backed by a $2M+ Make-A-Wish record. Goody has charity brands in the catalog but no comparable story.

## Where Snappy is exposed

1. **Recipient sentiment** (see the review split above). The sharpest wedge available.
2. **The down round and the layoffs.** A 30% cut and a halved valuation are public. Never say this to a prospect as an attack — it reads as desperate. It matters because it explains the quiet roadmap, and because an enterprise buyer doing diligence will find it themselves.
3. **Slack/Teams behind a $2,000 wall.**
4. **No MCP, no agentic surface.** A 2023 ChatGPT plugin is not the same thing and is arguably deprecated.
5. **Only ~40 HRIS integrations** against Goody's 200+. This was Snappy's advantage until June 2026 and it is now Goody's.
6. **"It skews corporate."** The most consistent complaint about the catalog across every review source.
7. **Employee sentiment.** **[unverified]** RepVue 2.8/5 from sales reps. A weak sales org is a recruitable sales org, and it is also why deals slip.
