# Moore (wearemoore.com) — Prospect Research for OpenTag

*Research date: 2026-08-30. Prepared for Tony Kam, Shelden Shi and Wilson Nguyen. Sources: wearemoore.com (home, /services, /moore-news, /blog, /careers, press releases), PRNewswire, Microsoft Customer Stories, The NonProfit Times, NonProfit PRO, ZipRecruiter/Mediabistro/Indeed job postings, Glassdoor, ZoomInfo, Owler, Tracxn. Read `opentag.md` and `competitors.md` first — this file assumes both. Items marked **[unverified]** come from a single third-party aggregator and could not be cross-checked against a primary source.*

> **Bottom line up front.** Moore is a ~5,000-person, privately held, Microsoft-aligned holding company assembled from roughly 33 acquired businesses. It is not OpenTag's current customer profile — every one of the six logos on OpenTag's homepage is an early-stage technical startup, and Moore is none of those things. But it is a legitimate opportunity for three specific reasons that will not all be true again: a brand-new CIO with an explicit AI mandate who is 30 days into the job, a documented post-merger knowledge-fragmentation problem that maps exactly onto the self-writing wiki, and a headcount at which every seat-priced competitor becomes absurd. Three things must be true for it to close: OpenTag has to open Microsoft Teams, has to scope the pilot away from donor PII until SOC 2 Type II lands, and has to beat Microsoft 365 Copilot, which is the real incumbent here — not Claude Tag.

## 3. The marketing operation

This is a mature, conventional, high-output B2B marketing machine — not a growth-hacking one.

**Owned content.** An active blog (posts run 1–11 minutes, weekly-ish cadence, heavily donor-strategy and product-led — recent titles include "Why Good Donors Go Quiet," "The Best Donor Intelligence Tools for Small- to Mid-Size Nonprofits," "The Mid-Level Donor Problem No One Talks About"), a webinar program, and a white-paper/case-study library. The blog is visibly doing product-led SEO for SimioAccelerate against the "donor intelligence tools" category.

**Earned and PR.** A heavy PRNewswire cadence — the newsroom runs to **13 pages of releases** (on the order of 100+ over its life), with 9 in the eight months of 2026 alone. Coverage lands in The NonProfit Times, NonProfit PRO, PIworld, MarTech Series and trade syndication. They chase and publicise awards: 11 DMAW MAXI Awards and 10 Communicator Awards of Excellence in 2026.

**Events.** AFP ICON is their launch stage (SimioAccelerate debuted there). The CEO led a panel at **CES 2026** on AI's impact in underserved communities — note that a nonprofit-fundraising CEO putting herself on a CES AI panel is a deliberate positioning move, not an accident.

**Partner co-marketing.** The Microsoft relationship is the centrepiece. Moore is a **Microsoft Elevate Partner**, SimioAccelerate was "developed in collaboration with Microsoft," and Microsoft publishes a Moore/CHOP customer story on microsoft.com. Moore gets Microsoft's brand credibility and co-sell motion; Microsoft gets a nonprofit-vertical proof point.

**Read for the pitch:** Moore's marketing team produces a lot of recurring, deadline-driven, multi-stakeholder output — press releases, award submissions, webinars, blog posts, case studies, campaign reporting — across an organisation stitched together from dozens of acquisitions. That is precisely the workload shape OpenTag's routines feature was built for, and it is a far better first use case than anything client-facing.

**Three reads from this table:**

1. **Moore is a Teams shop, not a Slack shop.** OpenTag's Teams support is invite-only. Everything else in this document is contingent on that changing. If the founders will not open Teams for this account, the honest recommendation is to pass and spend the cycles on the Claude Tag bill-shock cohort instead.
2. **Four project-management tools in one job description is a symptom.** Workfront *and* Smartsheet *and* MS Project *and* Asana in a single posting means different acquired units never converged. Work state lives in four places, and nobody has a single view of it. That is an argument to make out loud.
3. **The Microsoft relationship cuts both ways.** It makes an Azure-adjacent, Teams-native pitch credible. It also means **Microsoft 365 Copilot is sitting on the table with a partner discount attached** — see §7.

## 5. The opening: what changed in the last five weeks

On **2026-07-29** Moore announced three executive appointments:

- **Shamyo Chatterjee — Chief Information Officer.** New hire, external. Background: enterprise technology leadership and digital transformation at Silicon Valley companies and high-growth enterprises; "experience building enterprise AI organizations, modernizing technology platforms and leading large-scale business transformation." His own quote: *"I'm excited to join the organization at such an important point in its evolution and help accelerate our technology strategy, **expand the responsible use of AI** and continue building innovative solutions."*
- **Steve Harrison — promoted to Chief Revenue Officer** (previously President; he is the Moore executive quoted in Microsoft's CHOP case study). Owns "enterprise revenue strategy, business development, strategic partnerships."
- **Ryan Katz — President, Agency at Moore.** Mandate: "a more unified client experience, ensuring organizations can seamlessly access the full breadth of Moore's expertise through **one connected partnership**." His quote: *"Clients don't think about individual capabilities. They think about outcomes. Our responsibility is to deliver a unified experience that brings together the right expertise at the right time."*

**Read:** A brand-new CIO with a written AI mandate is roughly 30 days into the job as of today. New CIOs buy inside their first two quarters — that is when the budget is unspent, the incumbent stack is not yet theirs to defend, and a visible early win is worth more than a safe one. Simultaneously, a newly appointed agency president has publicly committed to solving cross-unit fragmentation. Those two mandates, held by two different people, both point at the same product. **This window is open now and will be substantially narrower by Q1 2027.**

## 6. Where OpenTag actually fits

### The problem OpenTag solves at Moore

Moore is ~33 companies wearing one name. Glassdoor reviewers describe the consequence directly: *"a serious lack of communication about strategy, financials, execution, and performance, with many disparate pockets of leadership advocating for competing priorities, making it hard for teams or individuals to know what is going on or why,"* alongside an acknowledgement that *"the company is taking steps to coordinate better across different departments but there is a lot of work yet to do."* Moore rates 3.4/5 overall with 49% recommending, across only 23 reviews — a small sample, so treat the number as weak and the *theme* as strong, because it is corroborated by Ryan Katz's own "one connected partnership" mandate and by four project-management tools in one job posting.

That is not an "AI assistant" problem. It is a **knowledge-fragmentation problem**, and the self-writing wiki is the only product in OpenTag's competitive set with no direct equivalent (see `competitors.md`, cross-cutting read 4).

### The four value arguments, ranked by strength

**1. The wiki, not the agent.** Institutional knowledge at Moore lives in the heads of people who joined through an acquisition, in Teams threads, and across four PM tools. A wiki that writes itself out of how the company actually operates — sourced to the thread the decision was made in, revising with a diff when the decision changes — is the answer to Katz's mandate and to the Glassdoor complaint. Lead with this. It is also the argument least likely to be confused with something Moore already sells.

**2. Pricing shape at 5,000 people.** This is OpenTag's strongest number and Moore is the best possible illustration of it. Per `competitors.md`: Dust is $24–120/seat/mo; Glean is ~$50+/seat/mo **[unverified]**; Microsoft 365 Copilot is ~$30/user/mo. At 5,000 people that is **$1.5M–$7.2M a year before anyone does any work.** OpenTag charges for work, not for seats. Do the honest arithmetic before the meeting — at Moore's scale the credit consumption will be large and the Enterprise plan is the only sane vehicle — but the *shape* argument is devastating and it is the one thing OpenTag has that no competitor can match without repricing their entire business.

**3. Recurring agency operations.** Weekly client status reporting, campaign briefs, mailing-schedule status, award submissions, RFP and proposal response, "what did we run for this client last year and what did it return." All recurring, all deadline-driven, all currently assembled by hand from four systems. This is the routines feature's home turf: *"same client status report, every Monday at 9:00, posted in this channel with the receipts attached."*

**4. Permission-following, not workspace ingestion.** Moore processes more than half of all nonprofit donations in the country and handles healthcare-adjacent donor data for CHOP. "Invited channels only," "permissions follow the person who asked," and "approval before anything leaves" are not marketing lines to this buyer — they are the first three questions security will ask. OpenTag's answers are unusually good and should be volunteered, not defended.

### What OpenTag should NOT say

**Do not position OpenTag anywhere near fundraising, donors, or campaign execution.** Moore sells SimioAccelerate — an agentic platform whose AI agents "orchestrate, automate and execute the entire process of effective fundraising." If OpenTag's pitch smells like a competitor to Moore's own flagship AI product, the conversation ends in the first meeting. OpenTag is **internal operations infrastructure for the people who build Moore's products and run Moore's agency.** Say that explicitly and early. If anything, congratulate them on SimioAccelerate and mean it — it is a genuine reason to believe this buyer understands agents already and will not need the category explained.

## 8. How the founders should run this

**Target, in order:**

1. **Shamyo Chatterjee, CIO** — economic buyer, holds the AI mandate, 30 days in. Highest ceiling, most security friction. He is on X as `@shamyoc`.
2. **Ryan Katz, President, Agency** — the champion. His public mandate is cross-unit fragmentation, which is the wiki's use case verbatim. Lowest friction, controls a real budget, and can sponsor a pilot without a company-wide security review.
3. **Steve Harrison, CRO** — cares about proposal and RFP turnaround, and about what the agency can promise clients. A secondary path if 1 and 2 stall.
4. Gretchen Littlefield, CEO — public AI evangelist, CES panellist. Do not cold-open here; use her as air cover once a champion exists.

**The recommended sequence:**

1. **Open with Katz, not the CIO.** Reference his own "clients don't think about individual capabilities" quote back to him and connect it to the internal version of the same problem: neither do employees. Ask one question — *how does someone at Moore find out what a different Moore company already did for the same client?* — and let him answer it.
2. **Lead the demo with the wiki.** Not the agent, not the eighty models. The demo that lands is: a decision gets made in a Teams thread, the wiki page updates itself with a diff and a link back to the thread. That is the thing Moore cannot buy anywhere else.
3. **Propose a scoped pilot, not a purchase.** One team — agency ops, marketing, or communications — 60 to 90 days, no donor PII in scope, one named recurring routine (the weekly client status report) plus the wiki. Free trial covers most of it; that removes procurement from the critical path entirely.
4. **Bring the seat arithmetic to the CIO conversation, written down.** 5,000 people × the per-seat alternatives, against a credit estimate for the pilot team's actual work. Make the shape visible on one page.
5. **Make the Teams commitment explicit and dated.** Either Moore is the Teams design partner with a date attached, or say plainly that Slack is the supported surface today and let them decide. Do not let ambiguity here run past the second meeting — it is the one thing that cannot be fixed later.

**Two questions to get answered early, because they change the plan:**

- Does any part of Moore run on Slack? A digitally native acquired unit — Moore Digital, launched 2022 — is the most likely candidate. If yes, that is the beachhead and the Teams blocker moves off the critical path.
- Is Microsoft already in the building with Copilot? Given Elevate Partner status, assume yes until told otherwise, and ask directly rather than discovering it in month three.

## 9. What we could not establish

Named honestly, because these gaps affect the plan:

- **Whether Moore has already deployed Microsoft 365 Copilot.** Nothing public either way. This is the highest-value unknown in the file.
- **Whether any Moore business unit uses Slack.** No public signal. Teams is the only chat tool named in job postings.
- **Actual revenue.** Estimates span $680M to $1.7B. Do not quote a figure to this prospect.
- **The complete list of the ~33 operating companies.** Moore has consolidated the brand and no longer publishes a roster. Confirmed units: Moore Digital (launched Feb 2022), Moore RMG (ex-Merkle Response Management Group, Jan 2023), Production Solutions/PS Digital (merged 2016), Southwest Publishing & Mailing / Southwest Caging (2015), Worcester Envelope (2021), Tri State Envelope (2021).
- **Headcount split** between production/mail/caging labour and knowledge workers. This matters a great deal for credit-consumption modelling — a lettershop operator is not an OpenTag user, and the addressable internal population is likely far below 5,000.
- **Whether Moore has an internal AI policy or an approved-tools list.** A new CIO with a "responsible use of AI" mandate very often writes one in month two. Worth asking about directly; if one is being drafted right now, being in the room while it is written is worth more than the pilot.
