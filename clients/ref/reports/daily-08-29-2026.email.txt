What this means: Anthropic just published your argument. The playbook opens with "code is no longer the bottleneck, your SDLC is" and moves human attention "from reading every line to judging intent and risk at the gates." That is your essay with Anthropic's name on it, and it is the single best piece of third-party air cover you will get this year. But read the second half. Their prescribed answer is markdown artifacts committed to git: intent.md, spec.md, plan.md. That is exactly the "markdown in the repo" approach you already wrote a comparison page against. Anthropic has told the whole market to do the thing you argue is not enough. Your move is not to disagree with the playbook. It is to say yes, and here is what happens when plan.md gets to 4,000 lines and your designer cannot open it. One more gift buried in there: the playbook asks teams to measure "how often the merged diff still matches the committed plan.md." That is plan blame, which is already on your roadmap, now an Anthropic-recommended metric.
