**A lead is a named person with a public profile and something specific to open with.** A company name with no contact path is evidence, not a lead. Reading the commit history on the code rows is what turned twelve companies into twelve named engineers, and that step is now standard for any competitor that sells an API.

**Confidence tiers.** **A** = someone said it or the artefact proves it. **B** = strong circumstantial. **C** = they pay attention to Deepgram. C is a lead, not a customer. Sentiment does not matter: a happy Deepgram customer with a public profile is as good a lead as an unhappy one, and usually easier to reach.

## What this pull did not find

- **Nobody is shopping for a Deepgram alternative on X.** A 30-day search for "deepgram alternative", "switching from deepgram" and "moving off deepgram" returned **zero** posts. Deepgram is not leaking customers through public dissatisfaction, which means Sprag has to win on price and on the products Deepgram does not sell rather than on unhappiness.
- **The Wayback churn diff did not work.** Deepgram's customers page is rendered in the browser, so the archived captures from Jan 2025, Aug 2025, Jan 2026 and Mar 2026 contain no customer names. The "which logo disappeared" signal is unavailable for this competitor. Firecrawl's structured extract on the live page works, so from now on we snapshot it ourselves each week and diff our own captures.
- **Only 1 of 60 LinkedIn job ads matching "Deepgram" actually named Deepgram.** The other 59 were LinkedIn's fuzzy AI search. They are not in this sheet.
- **G2 anonymises the company on every review pulled.** Reviewer name, job title, company size and industry are real; the employer is not published. Rows say so rather than guessing.
